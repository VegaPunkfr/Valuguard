# Conversion Logic — Ghost Tax Commercial Strategy

## The Rep-Free Conversion System

Ghost Tax converts without humans in the loop. No sales reps, no SDRs, no demos, no calls. The system must do the work that a salesperson would do: build trust, demonstrate value, create urgency, handle objections, and close.

**What replaces human sales:**
- The free scan replaces the discovery call
- The scan results replace the demo
- The pricing page replaces the proposal
- The sample report replaces the proof-of-concept
- The methodology page replaces the sales engineer
- The checkout replaces the contract negotiation
- The report itself replaces the account manager (for Rail B upsell)

Every one of these must be better than its human equivalent at this price point. A €490 product cannot justify a 30-minute sales cycle — but it can justify a 5-minute self-serve experience that's more convincing than any call.

## Conversion Funnel: Stage by Stage

### Stage 1: Visit → Free Scan

**Visitor arrives** (from LinkedIn content, SEO, outbound email, referral).

**What they see:** A landing page that communicates in 5 seconds: "Find out how much your company overspends on IT vendors. Free scan. No system access needed."

**Action required:** Enter company domain.

**Conversion target:** 25-35% of visitors enter a domain.

**What kills conversion here:**
- Too much text before the scan input
- Asking for email before the scan (gate = death)
- Unclear value proposition — "decision intelligence" means nothing to a first-time visitor
- Page load time >2 seconds
- Any mention of "schedule a call" or "request a demo"

**What drives conversion here:**
- One input field (domain), one button ("Scan")
- Immediate result (scan runs in 10-30 seconds with streaming progress)
- Social proof if available (even "238 companies scanned this month" is enough)
- Specific, concrete language: "We'll identify your IT vendors, estimate your spend, and flag where you're overpaying"

---

### Stage 2: Free Scan → Exposure Teaser

**Visitor sees scan results.** This is the most critical moment in the funnel.

**What they see:**
- Company name and basic profile confirmed ("we know who you are")
- 2-4 vendors detected with logos ("we found real things")
- Headline metrics: estimated annual IT spend range, estimated waste percentage
- A blurred or partial view of full findings ("there's more")
- Clear CTA: "Get Full Decision Pack — €490 — Delivered in 48 hours"

**Conversion target:** 5-8% of scan completions click through to checkout.

**What kills conversion here:**
- Scan results are wrong (wrong company, wrong vendors) — trust destroyed instantly
- Too much given away (if the free scan answers all questions, why pay?)
- Too little shown (if scan looks empty or generic, no curiosity created)
- No specificity — generic results that could apply to any company
- Teaser feels manipulative rather than genuinely incomplete
- Price not visible on this page

**What drives conversion here:**
- Correct vendor detection — even 3 correct vendors creates a "how did they know?" moment
- Specific numbers: "Estimated annual IT spend: €3.2M-4.1M" (not "your company may be overspending")
- The gap between what's shown and what's promised: "We identified 7 more vendors and 5 cost anomalies in the full analysis"
- Price shown immediately next to value: "€490 for the full Decision Pack" next to "Estimated addressable savings: €180K-340K"
- No friction: CTA goes directly to checkout, not to a form or a calendar

---

### Stage 3: Exposure Teaser → Checkout

**Visitor decides to pay.**

**What they see:**
- Stripe checkout (clean, trusted, recognizable)
- Order summary: "Ghost Tax Decision Pack — [Company Name] — €490"
- Delivery promise: "Delivered within 48 hours to your email"
- What's included (bullet list, 5-6 items)
- Trust signals: Stripe security, money-back guarantee if applicable

**Conversion target:** 60-75% of checkout initiations complete payment.

**What kills conversion here:**
- Requiring account creation before payment
- Asking for information beyond email + payment (no phone, no company size dropdown, no "how did you hear about us")
- Hidden fees or surprise VAT (show total including tax upfront)
- Checkout on a different domain than the main site
- No clear delivery timeline
- Payment methods too limited (must accept cards; ideal to accept SEPA for DACH)

**What drives conversion here:**
- One-page checkout, no steps
- "Pay once, no subscription" clearly stated
- Company name pre-filled from scan
- Delivery email pre-filled if captured during scan
- Total price visible including VAT from the first screen

---

### Stage 4: Checkout → Report Delivery

**Buyer waits for the report.**

**What happens:**
- Immediate confirmation email: "Your Decision Pack for [Company] is being prepared. Delivery within 48 hours."
- Optional: progress update at 24 hours ("Analysis in progress — we've identified 12 vendors so far")
- Report delivered via email as PDF + link to web version

**Conversion concern:** Buyer's remorse. The gap between payment and delivery is where regret lives.

**What kills the experience here:**
- No confirmation email (buyer wonders if payment went through)
- Silence for 48 hours (buyer forgets or loses interest)
- Delivery to spam folder
- Report that looks different from the sample/teaser

**What drives satisfaction here:**
- Instant confirmation with clear timeline
- Progress update that shows work is happening (even if automated)
- Report that exceeds the teaser promise — more vendors, more detail, more specific than expected
- Clean, professional PDF that looks like it came from a premium consultancy

---

### Stage 5: Report → Rail B Upsell (Post-Purchase Conversion)

**Buyer reads the report.** This is where Rail B sells itself.

**How the report creates Rail B demand:**

1. **The findings create internal pressure.** A report showing €340K in waste creates a mandate to act. Acting requires a plan. Rail B IS the plan.

2. **The confidence gaps invite upgrade.** "5 of these 12 findings are HIGH confidence. Want us to validate the remaining 7 with deeper analysis? That's what the Stabilization Package does."

3. **The action items are specific but complex.** "Renegotiate your Salesforce contract at renewal (April 2027) — estimated savings €45K-70K." The buyer reads this and thinks: "I need a negotiation brief, not just a tip."

4. **The internal circulation effect.** CFO forwards report to CTO. CTO says "this is useful but we need the vendor-by-vendor playbook." CFO looks at Rail B pricing. €4,990 for the playbook vs. hiring a consultant for €50K. Easy.

**Rail B offer placement:**
- In the report itself: a section titled "Next Steps" with Rail B described (not as a sales pitch — as a logical continuation)
- Follow-up email 7 days after delivery: "You've had a week with your Decision Pack. If you're ready to act on the findings, the Stabilization Package builds your execution roadmap. [Link]"
- Follow-up email 21 days after delivery: "Companies that act on Decision Pack findings within 30 days capture 3x more savings than those that wait. [Link to Rail B]"

**What kills the upsell:**
- Report was thin or inaccurate — no trust for a higher-ticket purchase
- Follow-up feels aggressive ("buy now!" language)
- Rail B scope is unclear — buyer doesn't know what they'd get
- No price transparency — "contact us for pricing" on Rail B is self-serve death

**Conversion target:** 10-12% of Rail A buyers purchase Rail B within 30 days.

---

### Stage 6: The Viral Loop

**The report circulates.**

**How it spreads:**
- CFO sends executive summary to CEO and board → board member mentions it at another company's board meeting
- CTO sends vendor list to IT managers → IT manager shares with peer at industry event
- Finance team uses the cost data in budget planning → data gets referenced in internal presentations → presentation shared externally

**Design for circulation:**
- Executive summary fits on one page and makes sense without context
- Every page has the Ghost Tax URL and "Get your company's analysis at ghost-tax.com"
- Charts and tables are screenshot-friendly (clean, high contrast, no tiny text)
- PDF metadata includes Ghost Tax branding

**Viral conversion path:**
New person sees report → visits ghost-tax.com → runs free scan on THEIR company → sees results → buys Rail A → their report circulates → repeat.

**Target:** Each report generates 0.3-0.5 new scans within 60 days. At 5% scan-to-purchase conversion, every 10 reports generate 1-2 new organic purchases.

## Where Prospects Drop and Why

| Drop Point | Why They Leave | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page → no scan | Value prop unclear; page too cluttered; looks like spam | Simplify to one message + one input field |
| Scan started → abandoned | Scan takes too long; loading state boring | Stream results in real-time; show progress |
| Scan complete → no checkout click | Results wrong or too generic; price too high for perceived value; not the buyer (researcher, not CFO) | Improve scan accuracy; sharpen teaser value |
| Checkout initiated → not completed | Friction in form; surprise pricing; no trust signals; payment method unavailable | Strip checkout to minimum; show trust badges; add SEPA for DACH |
| Report delivered → no engagement | Report went to spam; buyer forgot; report quality disappointing | Deliverability optimization; progress emails; report quality above expectation |
| Report read → no Rail B | Findings too thin; no clear next step; Rail B pricing hidden | Report must surface enough value to justify deeper engagement; include Rail B details in report |

## Urgency: Cost-of-Delay, Not Artificial Scarcity

**Never use:**
- "Only 5 spots left this month" (transparent lie for a digital product)
- "Price increases next week" (erodes trust)
- "Limited time offer" (commodity tactic)
- Countdown timers (associated with scam products)

**Always use:**
- **Cost-of-delay framing:** "Every month of unoptimized IT spend costs your company an estimated €28K in unnecessary costs. A 48-hour analysis costs €490."
- **Contract timing urgency:** "Your Salesforce renewal is likely in Q2. Renegotiating after renewal means waiting another year."
- **Budget cycle urgency:** "Most IT budgets lock in September-November. Decision Packs delivered now give you time to act before the next budget cycle."
- **Competitive urgency:** "Your competitors are already optimizing. SaaS spend optimization grew 90% in 2025."

These are real urgency signals, not manufactured pressure. They work because they're true.

## Trust Surface Before Checkout

What the visitor must see or experience before they'll pay:

1. **Proof of capability:** Free scan correctly identifies their vendors (they EXPERIENCED the product)
2. **Proof of methodology:** Methodology page or "How it works" section explains the approach
3. **Proof of deliverable:** Sample report shows exactly what €490 buys
4. **Proof of legitimacy:** Professional website, real company, identifiable founder
5. **Proof of safety:** Stripe checkout (not a random payment form), clear refund terms
6. **Proof of relevance:** Scan results are specific to THEIR company, not generic

**Minimum viable trust:** Items 1, 5, and 6 are non-negotiable. A visitor who runs a scan, sees correct results, and checks out via Stripe will buy. Items 2, 3, and 4 increase conversion rate but aren't blockers.

## Conversion Metrics That Matter

### Track These

| Metric | Why It Matters | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Scan completion rate | Measures top-of-funnel engagement | >80% of started scans complete |
| Scan-to-checkout rate | Measures teaser effectiveness | 5-8% |
| Checkout completion rate | Measures payment friction | 60-75% |
| Report open rate | Measures delivery effectiveness | >90% |
| Rail B conversion (30-day) | Measures report quality and upsell mechanism | 10-12% |
| Viral coefficient | Measures report circulation effect | 0.3-0.5 new scans per report |
| Time from scan to purchase | Measures decision speed | Median <24 hours |
| Cost per scan (paid traffic) | Measures acquisition efficiency | <€5 |

### Ignore These

| Vanity Metric | Why It's Noise |
|---|---|
| Total website visitors | Meaningless without scan conversion |
| Email list size | No outbound email list strategy at this stage |
| Social media followers | Doesn't correlate with revenue |
| Page views per session | More pages ≠ closer to purchase |
| Bounce rate (homepage) | Irrelevant if scan CTA is above fold |
| Time on site | Could mean engaged OR confused |

## Self-Serve Constraints

### What Works Without Calls
- Rail A sales (€490 impulse, fully self-serve)
- Free scan delivery and teaser
- Rail B upsell via report + automated follow-up emails
- Customer support via email for report questions
- Refund processing via Stripe

### What Doesn't Work Without Calls (Yet)
- Rail C sales (€20K+ requires relationship, but not necessarily calls — could be email-based)
- Complex objection handling for enterprise prospects (mitigate with comprehensive FAQ/methodology page)
- Custom scope negotiation (standardize the offer to eliminate negotiation)
- Partner/reseller conversations (defer until post-product-market-fit)

### Design Principle
If a conversion step requires a human, either automate it or eliminate it. If it can't be automated or eliminated, it doesn't belong in the current funnel. Add it later when revenue justifies hiring.
