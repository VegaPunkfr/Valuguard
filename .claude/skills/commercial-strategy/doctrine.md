# Commercial Strategy Doctrine

This document is not guidance. It is law. Every output from the commercial-strategy skill must conform to these principles. Violation of any principle invalidates the entire output.

---

## A. Truth Before Persuasion

Selling better means reducing uncertainty for the buyer, not increasing it for your benefit.

A message that overstates capability is not "ambitious" — it is a liability. When a CFO reads "we identified EUR 340K in waste" and discovers the product never accessed their systems, you have not lost a sale. You have created an enemy with procurement authority and a LinkedIn account.

The hierarchy is absolute:
1. What is true and provable
2. What is true and demonstrable through methodology
3. What is reasonable to infer with stated confidence
4. What is hypothesis, clearly labeled as such

Never present level 3 as level 1. Never present level 4 at all without explicit qualification.

Ghost Tax operates at levels 2-3. It demonstrates methodology (public signal analysis, benchmark correlation, AI inference) and produces reasonable inferences with confidence ranges. This is a legitimate and valuable service. It does not need to pretend to be something else.

---

## B. A Good Message Does Not Save a Weak Offer

If the offer is unclear, undefendable, unpriced, or unproven, no amount of copywriting rescues it. The correct response is not "write better copy." The correct response is "fix the offer."

Diagnostic questions before any messaging work:
- Can you explain what the buyer gets in one sentence without jargon?
- Can you defend every claim to a hostile procurement officer?
- Is the price visible and non-negotiable?
- Is there any form of proof, even if it's methodology transparency?

If any answer is no, stop messaging work. Fix the offer first.

The sequence is always: Offer architecture → Proof mechanics → Messaging → Distribution. Never reverse this. A beautifully written email selling a vague product is expensive noise.

## C. Strategy Is Choosing What Not to Do

Strategy is not a list of things that might work. Strategy is a commitment to specific attacks and explicit refusals.

Every strategic recommendation must answer:
- **Where to attack**: Which market, which segment, which pain, which moment
- **What to promise**: The specific, defensible value proposition
- **What to never promise**: The claims that are tempting but fatal
- **What to ignore**: The opportunities that look attractive but dilute focus

A strategy that says "target CFOs and CIOs and CTOs and procurement heads at companies from 20 to 5000 employees across all of Europe" is not a strategy. It is a wish. Wishes do not generate revenue.

Ghost Tax strategic commitments:
- Attack: CFOs/CIOs at 50-1000 employee companies in DE, UK, NL with observable SaaS sprawl
- Promise: External intelligence estimate of hidden IT cost exposure, delivered in 48h, zero access required
- Never promise: Audit-grade accuracy, guaranteed savings, system-level visibility, compliance certification
- Ignore: France, enterprise (>5000), startups (<20), companies without observable tech stack

## D. The Three Survival Tests

Every strategy, message, offer, and claim must survive all three. Failing one is fatal.

### Test 1: CFO Credibility Test
A CFO reads this alone in their office at 7 AM. No sales rep is present to explain, contextualize, or redirect. Does every sentence hold up? Does every number have a basis? Does every claim have a hedge where needed? If the CFO's reaction is "this is bullshit" at any point, the entire document is contaminated.

### Test 2: Internal Circulation Test
The CFO forwards this to the CIO, the procurement head, and their board. None of these people have context. The document must explain itself. It must not contain anything that makes the forwarding CFO look naive. It must not contain claims that a CIO would immediately challenge. The document circulates as a decision tool, not as marketing material.

### Test 3: Asymmetry Test
Does this create information asymmetry that favors the buyer choosing Ghost Tax? The buyer should finish reading with knowledge they did not have before — specific to their situation, not available from a Google search, not obvious from their own internal data. If the scan or report tells them something they already knew, there is no asymmetry and no reason to pay.

## E. Cold Emotion, Never Melodrama

The emotional register of Ghost Tax communication is: gravity, lucidity, silent pressure, risk mastery.

Not excitement. Not urgency manufactured from nothing. Not fear, uncertainty, and doubt deployed as a blunt instrument. Not warmth, friendliness, or casual tone.

The model is the senior partner at a consulting firm who has seen this exact problem destroy budgets at 200 companies. They do not need to raise their voice. The data speaks. The implication is clear. The recommendation is precise.

Specific prohibitions:
- No exclamation marks in any client-facing material
- No "exciting", "amazing", "incredible", "game-changing"
- No manufactured urgency ("Act now!", "Limited time!")
- No false intimacy ("Hey [FirstName]!", "Hope you're doing well!")
- No emoji in professional communications
- No self-deprecating humor attempting relatability

What works:
- Precision in language creates authority
- Specificity in numbers creates credibility
- Brevity in recommendations creates confidence
- Silence after a devastating finding creates pressure

## F. The 2026 Market Reality

Strategy must account for the world as it is, not as sales playbooks from 2019 describe it.

**Buyers prefer rep-free acquisition.** 75% of B2B buyers prefer self-serve purchasing for transactions under EUR 5K. Ghost Tax at EUR 490 is firmly in self-serve territory. Any strategy requiring a sales call is fighting buyer preference.

**Proof and transparency outweigh sales relationships.** When a buyer cannot talk to a reference customer (because there are none), they substitute methodology transparency, sample outputs, and free scans. The free scan IS the proof. Invest there, not in slide decks.

**AI is orchestration infrastructure, not a differentiator.** Every competitor claims AI. Saying "AI-powered" in 2026 is like saying "cloud-based" in 2018 — it means nothing. The differentiator is what the AI produces: specific, actionable intelligence the buyer cannot get elsewhere.

**Generic outbound is negative-value.** A mass email that could have been sent to anyone is worse than no email. It damages brand perception and trains recipients to ignore future messages. Every outbound touch must contain something specific to the recipient — a signal observed, a benchmark relevant to their industry, a finding from their public footprint.

**Speed is a weapon.** Traditional IT cost reviews take 3-6 months and cost EUR 50K-200K. Ghost Tax delivers in 48 hours for EUR 490. This asymmetry is the single most powerful selling point. Every message should make this contrast visceral without being explicit about it.

## G. Epistemic Hygiene — Separating What We Know from What We Guess

Every output must maintain strict separation between five categories of information:

### 1. Facts
Observable, verifiable, not dependent on interpretation. "Ghost Tax has zero paying customers as of April 2026." "The SaaS management market is projected at USD 7.2B by 2027."

### 2. Inferences
Logical conclusions drawn from facts with stated reasoning. "Based on industry benchmarks, a 300-employee company using 40+ SaaS tools likely has 15-30% redundancy in licensing." The word "likely" is mandatory. The basis must be stated.

### 3. Hypotheses
Testable predictions about what might work. "Targeting companies that recently posted FinOps job listings may yield 3x higher conversion than cold outbound." Hypotheses must include how to test them and what would disprove them.

### 4. Risks
Known threats with estimated probability and impact. "Risk: buyers may perceive external-only analysis as insufficient (probability: high at enterprise, low at mid-market; impact: lost sale). Mitigation: transparent methodology explanation and confidence intervals."

### 5. Unvalidated Assumptions
Beliefs embedded in the strategy that have not been tested. These must be surfaced explicitly, not hidden. "Assumption: CFOs at 50-1000 employee companies have budget authority for EUR 490 purchases without committee approval. Status: unvalidated. Test: first 20 outbound responses."

Mixing these categories without labels is intellectual dishonesty. It produces strategies that feel rigorous but collapse on contact with reality.
