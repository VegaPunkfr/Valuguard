## 2. Phase 1: Proof Acquisition (Weeks 1-4)

**Objective**: Get 3 beta clients through the pipeline. Learn what works, what breaks, what surprises.

### 2.1 Beta Client Sourcing

Since Edith has no direct CFO network, beta clients must come from:

**Channel A — LinkedIn warm outreach (primary)**
- Identify 30 second-degree connections who are CFOs/CIOs at 50-500 employee companies in DACH/UK
- Post 2x/week about IT cost blind spots (no selling, pure insight)
- DM approach: "I'm building an external IT cost intelligence tool. Looking for 3 beta testers to run a free analysis. Takes zero access to your systems. Would you be open to being one?"
- Target acceptance: 10% = 3 beta clients from 30 outreach

**Channel B — Community/forum presence**
- Target: CFO-focused LinkedIn groups, FinOps Slack, Reddit r/CFO, Blind
- Post genuinely useful content about SaaS cost trends (12.2% inflation stat is gold)
- No selling. Build presence. Collect DMs.

**Channel C — Free scan as bait**
- The /intel endpoint is live. Push free scans to anyone who'll try it.
- Each free scan generates data AND gives the prospect a taste of the methodology
- If a free scan surfaces something interesting, that's the conversion moment

### 2.2 Beta Engagement Model

- **Price**: Free for first 3. In exchange: permission to use anonymized findings + a 2-paragraph written testimonial if satisfied.
- **Delivery**: Run the full pipeline. Treat it like a EUR 490 engagement. 48h turnaround.
- **Debrief**: Async. Send the report, ask 5 structured questions via email:
  1. What surprised you?
  2. What would you investigate further based on this?
  3. What would you have expected that wasn't there?
  4. Would you share this with your CIO? Board?
  5. What would you pay for this?

### 2.4 What We're Really Testing

- Does the report actually surprise CFOs? Or do they already know everything in it?
- Is 48h delivery a real differentiator or do they not care about speed at this price?
- Does the "no system access" angle remove friction or create skepticism?

- Do they share it internally? With whom?

## 3. Phase 2: First Outbound With Real Proof (Weeks 5-8)

**Prerequisite**: At least 1 beta client has confirmed the report was useful and provided a testimonial.

### 3.1 Apollo Targeting — DACH Priority

**Why DACH first:**
- EUR 590 pricing (20% premium)
- German CFOs are methodical — they value structured analysis
- Compliance culture means they're more likely to act on findings
- Less competition from US-based SaaS cost tools

**Apollo Search Parameters:**
```
Title: CFO, VP Finance, Finance Director, Leiter Finanzen, Kaufmännischer Leiter
Company size: 50-500 employees
Industry: Software, SaaS, Fintech, IT Services
Location: Germany, Austria, Switzerland
Funding: Series A through Series D (has money, burning SaaS spend)
```

**Credit Budget**: 385 credits/month. At ~1 credit per contact enrichment:
- Week 5-6: 100 contacts enriched, 50 emailed (best-fit only)
- Week 7-8: 100 contacts enriched, 50 emailed
- Reserve 185 credits for re-enrichment and follow-up research

### 3.2 Email Approach

**Subject lines to test (A/B):**
- "Your SaaS spend vs. {industry} benchmark"
- "What {company_name}'s IT contracts reveal from the outside"
- "3 things we noticed about {company_name}'s tech stack"

**Email body framework:**
1. One specific observation about their company (tech stack signal, recent vendor change, hiring pattern)
2. One relevant industry stat (SaaS inflation 12.2%, 42% companies cutting SaaS budgets)
3. The offer: "We ran a preliminary scan. Happy to share the summary — no system access needed, no call required."
4. Social proof: "We recently analyzed a {similar_size} {similar_industry} company and identified {X}% of spend worth investigating."

**Critical rule**: Every email must reference a real, specific observation. Never send generic templates.

### 3.3 Conversion Path

```
Apollo email → Free scan landing page → Scan runs → Results page
→ "Want the full report? EUR 590, delivered in 48h" → Stripe checkout
→ Report delivered → Follow-up: "Want to explore stabilization?" (Rail B tease)
```

No calls. No demos. No "let's schedule a chat." The product sells itself or it doesn't.

## 7. Kill Criteria

### Kill Signal 1: Zero Beta Interest (Week 3)
If 15+ outreach messages yield zero beta takers → the value proposition doesn't resonate. Pivot messaging before spending Apollo credits.

### Kill Signal 2: Beta Reports Get No Reaction (Week 5)
If 3 beta reports are delivered and zero testimonials / zero follow-up questions → the report doesn't deliver value. Redesign the report before going outbound.

### Kill Signal 3: Outbound Gets Zero Replies (Week 8)
200 emails, <1% reply rate → Either the targeting is wrong, the messaging is wrong, or the market doesn't want this. Diagnose before continuing.

### Kill Signal 4: Free Scans Don't Convert (Month 3)
50+ free scans, zero Rail A purchases → The gap between free and paid is too large, or the free scan gives away too much. Restructure the funnel.

### Kill Signal 5: Revenue Plateaus Below EUR 3K/month (Month 6)
If after 6 months of effort, monthly revenue is stuck below EUR 3K → the unit economics don't work for a solo founder. Consider: pivot to Rail B/C only (consulting model), find a co-founder with sales skills, or sunset the project.

## 8. Dependencies and Risks

### Critical Dependencies
1. **Exa API reliability**: The entire intelligence pipeline depends on Exa neural search. If Exa degrades, has outages, or changes pricing, the product breaks.
2. **AI model quality**: Report quality depends on Claude/GPT inference. Model degradation = report degradation.
3. **Stripe checkout**: Self-serve revenue requires frictionless checkout. Any payment friction kills conversion.
4. **Apollo data quality**: Outbound depends on accurate contact data. DACH data quality on Apollo is variable.

### Key Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-------------|--------|------------|
| Report doesn't surprise CFOs | 40% | Fatal | Beta feedback loop, iterate fast |
| Apollo DACH data quality is poor | 30% | High | Test with 50 contacts first, measure bounce rates |
| "No system access" creates skepticism | 25% | Medium | Lead with methodology transparency, offer free scan as proof |
| Competitor launches similar product | 15% | Medium | Speed advantage (already built), first-mover in DACH |
| Edith burns out (solo, no revenue) | 35% | Fatal | Set clear kill criteria, don't grind past month 6 without traction |
| EUR 49/month budget insufficient | 50% | Medium | Maximize organic channels, reinvest first revenue into tools |

### The One Risk That Matters Most
**Product-market fit uncertainty.** Everything else is solvable. If CFOs don't find the report valuable enough to pay EUR 490-590 for, no amount of outbound or content will fix it. Phase 1 exists solely to answer this question.
